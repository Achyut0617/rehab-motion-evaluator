# Jetson Edge Keeps Rehab Feedback Fast and Private

A Jetson Orin Nano (Super) running a nano-size YOLO-pose model as a TensorRT FP16 engine is the right foundation for this rehab app, and the evidence supports it on every axis that was asked about. On speed, the model alone takes about **4.4–4.6 ms per 640 px frame**. The full camera-to-feedback loop should take roughly **45–80 ms (calc)**. Streaming the same video to a cloud GPU from India should take roughly **180–260 ms at the median (calc)**, because the median round trip to the major cloud regions is already 108–158 ms. That difference matters clinically: motor-learning studies show that even tens of milliseconds of visual delay reduce implicit learning. Most of the failures you will meet on the Jetson are configuration mistakes rather than hardware faults, and each one leaves a fingerprint you can detect. The common ones are power-budget throttling (shown by the misleading "over-current" message), PyTorch or OpenCV wheels that silently replace JetPack's CUDA/GStreamer builds, TensorRT engines copied across versions, memory exhaustion on the 8 GB shared pool when an LLM shares the board, stale frames building up from the Wi-Fi IP camera, and microSD corruption. The hybrid design keeps video on the device and sends only a de-identified numeric summary to Gemini after the session. It runs fully offline, sends almost nothing upstream, and costs less than cloud GPU time once the board has run for about **430 hours (calc)**. It also shrinks the data that India's DPDP regime (penalties up to **₹250 crore**) applies to, although the free Gemini tier allows human review of prompts, so identifiers must be stripped. Two caveats limit the numbers. Use FP16, not INT8: INT8 cut YOLO26n-pose accuracy from 56.7 to 33.4 mAP. And camera-based joint angles agree with motion capture to only about **4–8°** even in a controlled side-on setup. No published benchmark of YOLOv8n-pose or MediaPipe Pose on an Orin Nano exists, so the project's own measurements will be the definitive figures.

**How to read the numbers.** A figure followed by a citation link is **sourced**. A figure tagged **(calc)** is my own arithmetic from sourced inputs plus stated assumptions. **(unverified)** marks a value that appears in the research notes but was not confirmed against a primary source. INR conversions assume **₹85/USD** and exclude import duty and GST, which raise Indian retail prices substantially.

## Most Jetson failures are misconfigurations, and each leaves a detectable fingerprint

### Power budgets, not heat, usually explain falling FPS

The Orin Nano does not start thermal throttling until about **99 °C (software) and 103 °C (hardware)**. It shuts down at 104.5–105 °C. When it does throttle on heat, it cuts clocks by **50%, 75% or 87.5%** depending on severity ([NVIDIA Jetson Linux Dev Guide r36.4](https://docs.nvidia.com/jetson/archives/r36.4/DeveloperGuide/SD/PlatformPowerAndPerformance/JetsonOrinNanoSeriesJetsonOrinNxSeriesAndJetsonAgxOrinSeries.html)). A dev kit with its stock heatsink rarely reaches those temperatures. If FPS drops while the board sits at 50–80 °C, the cause is almost always the **power limit of the current nvpmodel mode**. The on-screen warning "System throttled due to over-current" uses the same wording for heat and for power, and an NVIDIA moderator called it "misleading" in a thread where the board was only at 50–60 °C ([NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/system-throttled-due-to-overcurrent-while-running-tensorrt/286431)). In 15 W mode, the board throttles CPU/GPU by 50% when **average power exceeds 15 W** and by 87.5% when **instantaneous power exceeds 20 W**. It also throttles by 87.5% when **input voltage dips below about 4.5 V**. You can read each type of event from the `oc*_event_cnt` counters under the INA3221 hwmon node ([Dev Guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/jetson/archives/r36.4/DeveloperGuide/SD/PlatformPowerAndPerformance/JetsonOrinNanoSeriesJetsonOrinNxSeriesAndJetsonAgxOrinSeries.html)). If the under-voltage counter is climbing, suspect the power supply or cable. Rising average or instantaneous counters mean the workload is exceeding the mode's budget, and the fix is a higher power mode, not a repair.

The mode numbering trips people up. On the Orin Nano 8GB, mode **ID 0 is 15 W, ID 1 is 7 W and ID 2 is MAXN**. MAXN raises the GPU from 625 to **1020 MHz** and memory from 2133 to **3199 MHz**, but it exists only after you flash the `jetson-orin-nano-devkit-super` configuration with JetPack 6.2 or later ([Dev Guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/jetson/archives/r36.4/DeveloperGuide/SD/PlatformPowerAndPerformance/JetsonOrinNanoSeriesJetsonOrinNxSeriesAndJetsonAgxOrinSeries.html); [JetPack 6.2 Super Mode thread](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/jetpack-6-2-brings-super-mode-to-nvidia-jetson-orin-nano-and-jetson-orin-nx-modules/320344)). Ultralytics' generic advice to run `sudo nvpmodel -m 0` ([Ultralytics Jetson guide](https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/nvidia-jetson/)) therefore selects the *15 W* mode on an Orin Nano, not the fastest one. Always confirm the active mode with `nvpmodel -q --verbose`. Order also matters: once `jetson_clocks` has been run, **the nvpmodel mode cannot be changed without a reboot**. Since L4T 32.4, `jetson_clocks` also no longer sets the fan to maximum unless you add `--fan`. The default "quiet" fan profile turns the fan off entirely when the board is ≥70 °C below its trip point ([Dev Guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/jetson/archives/r36.4/DeveloperGuide/SD/PlatformPowerAndPerformance/JetsonOrinNanoSeriesJetsonOrinNxSeriesAndJetsonAgxOrinSeries.html)). Users also report that a dev kit got stuck in 25 W mode, and that on JetPack 7.2 the 25 W and MAXN SUPER options were missing from the GUI and did not persist across reboots ([forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/orin-nano-super-developer-kit-wont-switch-to-maxn-super-or-any-other-mode-stuck-on-25w/350500); [forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/25w-and-maxn-super-not-seen-in-jetpack-7-2/372627)). Your startup script should therefore set and verify the mode itself rather than trusting the desktop setting. For a box enclosure in a hot Indian clinic, use `jetson_clocks --fan` or the "cool" profile. Log tegrastats temperatures and GPU frequency to SQLite next to FPS so you can tell heat and power throttling apart afterwards. Heavy throttling cuts clocks to one-eighth, so a 10 ms inference could stretch to roughly **40–80 ms (calc, extrapolated from the clock-cut percentages and not measured)**.

### Eight gigabytes is shared by CPU, GPU, desktop and any LLM

The CPU and GPU draw on the same 8 GB, so the desktop, PyTorch's CUDA context, the Flask app and any local LLM all compete for one pool. Booting headless (`sudo systemctl set-default multi-user.target`) frees about **800–865 MB**, and dropping display windows (`show=False`) frees another **~200 MB** ([Jetson AI Lab](https://www.jetson-ai-lab.com/tutorials/ram-optimization/); [Ultralytics](https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/nvidia-jetson/)). Forum users report that zram alone ran out of memory on the 8 GB Orin Nano. One VILA 1.5 3B run locked up even with 20 GB of swap, Ollama services have been OOM-killed, and NVIDIA staff recommend keeping LLMs **under 4B parameters** on this board ([zram thread](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/zram-swap-on-jetson-orin-efficient-enough/277490); [VILA thread](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/vila-1-5-3b-on-jetson-orin-nano/293000); [Ollama OOM](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/ollama-service-failed-with-result-oom-kill/374647); [YOLO+LLM thread](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/jetson-orin-nano-deploying-yolo-with-llm/359823)). The out-of-memory signatures are a process ending with "Killed" (exit code 137), `oom-kill` entries in `dmesg` or `journalctl`, and CUDA out-of-memory errors even when free RAM looks available. Because the GPU draws on the same physical pool, swap can relieve CPU-side pages but cannot back GPU allocations (an inference from the unified-memory design, not stated in an NVIDIA document). That is the strongest argument for doing report generation with Gemini in the cloud rather than with an on-board LLM.

### The Python stack silently replaces Jetson's accelerated builds

Four mismatches account for most "it runs but it's slow" or "it won't import" problems. First, **MediaPipe on Jetson is CPU-only**. The PyPI aarch64 wheel installs ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/mediapipe/#files)), but the TFLite GPU delegate does not use the Jetson's CUDA GPU, and a user who built it from source still measured **under 5 FPS** for hand tracking on an Orin Nano ([NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/mediapipe-tensorflow-lite-gpu-delegate-on-jetson-orin-nano/296851)). Second, PyTorch wheels from PyPI or pytorch.org for generic ARM64 are **CPU-only builds**, so `torch.cuda.is_available()` returns False ([forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/in-pytorch-6-1-the-gpu-is-not-working/314143)). You need the Jetson wheels matched to your JetPack: cp38 for JetPack 5, cp310 for JetPack 6, cp312 with CUDA 13.0 wheels for JetPack 7.2 ([Ultralytics](https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/nvidia-jetson/)). NVIDIA's JetPack 6 torch wheels are built against NumPy 1.x and fail under NumPy 2 ([forum 370484](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/pytorch-from-jp6-cu126-on-jetson-orin-nano-jetpack-6-2-1-cuda-12-6-failing-due-to-numpy-mismatch/370484)), so pin `numpy<2`. Third, `pip install opencv-python` (or the `opencv-contrib-python` that MediaPipe pulls in) installs a build **without GStreamer** that shadows JetPack's apt OpenCV ([Ultralytics](https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/nvidia-jetson/)). Fourth, a TensorRT `.engine` file is tied to the **TensorRT version and GPU compute capability (sm_87)** it was built on ([TensorRT engine compatibility](https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/tensorrt/latest/inference-library/engine-compatibility.html)). Moving an engine to another device or JetPack version produces "Serialization assertion … Version tag does not match" or "magicTag" errors ([forum 309261](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/serialization-assertion-stdversionread-kserialization-version-failed-version-tag-does-not-match/309261); [forum 300099](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/error-code-1-serialization-serialization-assertion-plan-header-magictag-rt-kplan-magic-tag-failed/300099)). Ultralytics adds three pose-specific traps: dynamic-shape pose export fails with a Myelin "Could not find any implementation" error, so export with `dynamic=False`; TensorRT 10.3 (JetPack 6) cannot build INT8 engines; and `pip install tensorrt` fails on Tegra ([Ultralytics](https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/nvidia-jetson/)). Inside a virtual environment, TensorRT and apt OpenCV are visible only if you create the venv with `--system-site-packages`, and conda environments lose the TensorRT bindings ([forum 223360](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/cant-access-tensorrt-from-a-python-venv/223360/5); [forum 276829](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/installing-tensorrt-in-a-conda-env-on-jetson-orin-nano/276829)).

### Wi-Fi cameras and microSD cards fail slowly, then all at once

Latency that keeps growing on a network stream is a known Jetson symptom. It happens when the program consumes frames more slowly than they arrive, and the documented fixes are leaky queues and `appsink drop=true max-buffers=1 sync=false` in the GStreamer pipeline ([forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/jetson-nano-rtsp-stream-constant-increase-in-delay/169030/8); [forum 282046](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/increasing-latency-when-using-gstreamer-in-opencv/282046)). `CAP_PROP_BUFFERSIZE` is documented only for the V4L backend, not for GStreamer or FFmpeg ([forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/delay-with-gstreamer-in-opencv-3-4-2/67784)). For the Android IP Webcam's HTTP MJPEG stream, the robust pattern is a dedicated capture thread that keeps only the latest frame. On the reconnect side, if no frame arrives for about 2 s, the app should reopen the stream with exponential backoff and show a "camera offline" frame so the Flask MJPEG response never hangs. This is engineering judgement; the notes found no measured latency figures for phone cameras over Wi-Fi. Storage is the other slow-burn risk. Forum moderators describe **microSD corruption on the Orin Nano as a known issue** (reportedly improved in JetPack 5.1.4/6.1, not verified against the release notes) and recommend NVMe ([forum 315022](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/frequent-sd-card-issues-and-boot-failures-on-jetson-orin-nano-8gb-developer-kit/315022)). They also stress that **any writable filesystem, NVMe included, can corrupt on sudden power loss** ([forum 260041](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/the-integrity-of-the-jetson-file-storage-system/260041); [JetPack 7.2 NVMe report](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/jetson-orin-nano-repeated-ext4-nvme-filesystem-corruption-and-attempting-recovery-boot-on-jetpack-7-2/379702)). In Indian clinics with unreliable mains power, a UPS is part of the bill of materials.

## Edge feedback arrives roughly three times faster than a cloud round trip from India

### Latency is the decisive argument, and the motor-learning literature explains why

Ookla's 2026 measurements put India's **median latency to cloud regions at 108 ms (Azure), 114 ms (AWS), 115 ms (Google Cloud) and 158 ms (Oracle)**. Ninetieth-percentile jitter is 25.7 ms, and latency **degrades 4.0× under full load** ([Republic World summarising Ookla](https://www.republicworld.com/tech/india-s-5g-networks-can-t-yet-support-llms-despite-strong-upload-gains-says-report-2026-07-07-131523)). Here is how a cloud pipeline adds up. Frame capture takes about 33 ms, encoding 5–20 ms, and uploading a 40 KB JPEG at India's 15.75 Mbps median 5G upload about 20 ms. Add the 108–158 ms round trip, 5–15 ms of GPU inference and 5–10 ms to return and render the result, and the total is about **180–260 ms at the median, with 300–500+ ms spikes under load (calc)**. On the Jetson, the 33 ms frame interval, about 5–30 ms of pose inference and 5–15 ms of angle and UI work add up to about **45–80 ms (calc)**. The threshold matters because a visuomotor study comparing 25, 85 and 300 ms of feedback delay found that **cutting about 60 ms of latency raised implicit learning by about 50%**, and implicit learning is most sensitive below 100 ms ([PMC12062458](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12062458)). Feedback lags of 75 and 150 ms also slowed adaptation in a reaching task ([PMC5646192](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5646192)). Users perceive lag in graphical responses at around **100–200 ms** ([PMC3297075](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3297075)). The edge design meets the sub-100 ms target; the cloud design misses it before jitter is even counted. A "straighten your knee" cue that arrives 300 ms late refers to a movement phase the patient has already finished. One honest caveat: no clinical latency threshold specific to rehab was found. The 100 ms target is extrapolated from laboratory motor-learning studies.

### Keeping video on the device shrinks DPDP exposure but does not remove it

MeitY notified the final **DPDP Rules on 13 November 2025** (PIB dates it the 14th). Commencement is phased. The Data Protection Board provisions applied immediately, consent managers follow after 12 months, and most data-fiduciary duties follow after **18 months, around 13 May 2027**: notice and consent, security safeguards, breach intimation, data-principal rights and cross-border rules ([Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/view-point/meity-notifies-final-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/centre-notifies-dpdp-rules-implementation-planned-in-phases-spread-over-12-18-months-3798465)). A breach must be reported **within 72 hours**, and processing logs must be kept for **at least one year** ([Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/view-point/meity-notifies-final-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025)). Failing to keep reasonable security safeguards can draw up to **₹250 crore**, and failing to notify a breach up to ₹200 crore ([Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/data-privacy-breach-enforcement-penalties-under-the-dpdp-act); [dpdpa.com Schedule](https://dpdpa.com/theschedule.html)). For comparison, GDPR Article 9 treats health data, and biometric data used for identification, as special categories that need explicit consent or a health-care exception ([legislation.gov.uk Art. 9](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/9/adopted)). HIPAA's 2025 inflation-adjusted civil penalties cap at **$2,190,294 per year per identical provision** ([HIPAA Journal](https://www.hipaajournal.com/hhs-hipaa-penalties-2026-no-adjustment/); [AccountableHQ](https://www.accountablehq.com/post/hipaa-violation-fines-2025-updated-penalty-tiers-caps-and-requirements)). In every regime, never transmitting or storing patient video removes the highest-risk data category, removes the need for a cloud processor contract covering video, and avoids cross-border transfer questions. However, joint-angle telemetry linked to an ECHS card number is **still personal health data**. The Jetson therefore still needs a consent notice, access control, encryption at rest, one-year log retention and a breach plan. The hybrid step carries its own risk: under the **free Gemini API tier, Google may use prompts to improve its products, and human reviewers may read them**. Enabling Cloud Billing makes usage a paid service whose prompts are not used for product improvement ([Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms-archive/terms_05_20_25); [Gemini logging policy](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/logs-policy)). The report request must therefore exclude names, ECHS IDs, dates of birth and free-text notes. Use age bands and condition codes instead. Whether ECHS, as a Ministry of Defence scheme, falls under the DPDP s.17 government exemptions was not researched, and you should not assume it does.

### Offline operation matters most where ECHS beneficiaries live

TRAI's figures show **rural tele-density of 60.46% (March 2026) against roughly 135–149% in urban areas**, and rural wireline tele-density under 0.6% ([TRAI PR No.32 of 2026](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-03/PR_No.32of2026.pdf); [Indian Television on TRAI](https://indiantelevision.com/mam/india-crosses-1-09-billion-internet-subscribers-as-telecom-use-surges/)). Rural internet density is about **45 per 100 people against about 111 in urban areas** ([Free Press Journal citing TRAI](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/indias-telecom-subscriber-base-surpasses-12-billion-rural-users-drive-growth-trai)). Rural home rehab therefore depends on mobile uplink, the weakest link in the chain. A cloud-dependent coach stops mid-exercise when the network drops, the region suffers an outage or the API quota runs out. The edge device keeps working. Its failure modes are local (power, storage, camera) and can be mitigated with a UPS and NVMe. The right pattern is store-and-forward: queue the telemetry locally, generate the Gemini report when connectivity returns, and keep a template-based local report as a fallback for when Gemini is unreachable or rate-limited.

### Cost and energy both favour the edge for a single clinic

The **Jetson Orin Nano Super dev kit costs $249** (down from $499) and is rated at 67 sparse / 33 dense INT8 TOPS ([NVIDIA](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/autonomous-machines/embedded-systems/jetson-orin/nano-super-developer-kit); [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/nvidia-launches-new-usd249-ai-development-board-that-does-67-tops)). An AWS g4dn.xlarge (one T4) costs about **$0.579/h on demand in Mumbai**, according to third-party price trackers ([DoiT](https://www.doit.com/compute/spot/ap-south-1/g4dn.xlarge)). The board therefore equals about **430 cloud GPU-hours (calc)**. At 4 hours a day, 6 days a week, it pays for itself in about **4 months (calc)**. A home patient using it 1 hour a day would take about 14 months. If the cloud instance has to run 24×7 to be available, it costs about $417 a month and the Jetson breaks even in **about 18 days (calc)**. Spot instances (~$0.21/h) are cheaper but can be interrupted mid-session, so they are unsuitable. With peripherals, the edge bill of materials is about $300–350, which stretches break-even by about 1.3× (calc). On energy, the Jetson runs at **7–25 W** depending on mode ([NVIDIA](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/autonomous-machines/embedded-systems/jetson-orin/nano-super-developer-kit)). That is about **15 Wh per patient-hour at 15 W, or ₹0.12–0.20 per hour at an assumed ₹8/kWh (calc; the tariff is unsourced)**. A dedicated T4 is a 70 W card (unverified) inside a host server with data-centre overhead, so one patient per GPU lands at roughly **100–450 Wh per patient-hour (calc; the host share and PUE are unsourced assumptions)**. That is about an order of magnitude more, before counting the energy of the mobile network carrying the video. Cloud closes the gap only by batching many concurrent patients per GPU, which a single clinic cannot do.

The edge design has real downsides, and they deserve a fair hearing. The device itself must be secured with disk encryption (LUKS or SQLCipher) and per-user login. Each polyclinic box drifts unless there is an over-the-air update process. Improving the model is hard when video never leaves the device. A cloud-heavy design would make sense only if the clinic ran many concurrent streams on reliable fibre and needed central model retraining, and none of those conditions holds for an ECHS polyclinic or a home setting. An end-cloud split, with lightweight pose at the edge and analysis in the cloud, is also the architecture that rehab-system literature converges on ([Preprints.org](https://www.preprints.org/frontend/manuscript/8150d4bd5b115e584571fabf6fc8aae2/download_pub); [MDPI Electronics 12(16):3375](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/12/16/3375)).

Three conclusions follow from the table. First, TensorRT is the multiplier that justifies the Jetson: FP16 runs **3.4× (YOLO26n) to 4.7× (YOLOv8n) faster than PyTorch (calc)** on the same board. Second, at about 4.5 ms per frame the GPU is no longer the bottleneck. Camera I/O, Python pre/post-processing and the Flask encoder are. Third, the Pi 5 with Hailo-8L can reach real-time pose for about the same money, but only after an offline Dataflow Compiler step on an x86 host. For a student project that must ship, the Jetson's single on-device toolchain (PyTorch → ONNX → TensorRT) is the lower-risk path. On efficiency, the Orin Nano Super delivers about **9 FPS/W** (225 FPS ÷ 25 W mode cap) against under **2 FPS/W** for the Pi 5 CPU (calc; both use rated envelopes, not measured draw). No measured wall-power figure for a pose workload was found for any platform. NVIDIA's own Super-mode speed-ups of **1.4–1.7×** were published only for transformer models such as CLIP, DINOv2 and SAM2, not for YOLO ([NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-jetpack-6-2-brings-super-mode-to-nvidia-jetson-orin-nano-and-jetson-orin-nx-modules)).

MediaPipe's world landmarks give metric 3D coordinates, which reduce perspective error in angle calculations. That makes it the better *measurement* model when exactly one patient is in frame. Its weakness on this hardware is that it runs on the CPU. YOLO-pose is the better *engineering* model on a Jetson. It uses the GPU through TensorRT, handles a therapist in frame alongside the patient, and can be fine-tuned. Switching from YOLOv8n-pose to YOLO26n-pose costs nothing in size or API and gains 6.8 mAP. A sensible design keeps both: YOLO26n-pose TensorRT FP16 as the primary path and MediaPipe Lite as the CPU fallback when the engine fails to load.

### Precision: FP16 is free, INT8 is a trap for keypoints

| Precision (Orin Nano Super, YOLO26n detection, 640) | Latency | Speed-up vs PyTorch (calc) | mAP50-95 | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PyTorch | 15.60 ms | 1.0× | 0.479 | [Ultralytics Jetson](https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/nvidia-jetson/) |
| TensorRT FP32 | 7.53 ms | 2.1× | 0.477 | same |
| **TensorRT FP16** | **4.57 ms** | **3.4×** | **0.480** | same |
| TensorRT INT8 | 3.80 ms | 4.1× | 0.449 | same |
| **YOLO26n-pose (RTX PRO 6000)**: FP32 / FP16 / INT8 | 0.76 / **0.51** / 0.59 ms | — | **56.7 / 56.7 / 33.4** pose mAP | [Ultralytics TensorRT](https://docs.ultralytics.com/integrations/tensorrt/) |

On the Orin Nano Super, FP16 runs about **1.65× faster than TensorRT FP32 with no accuracy loss**. INT8 adds only about **1.2× more speed (calc)** at nano size and cost 3 mAP points on detection. For pose the damage is far worse: Ultralytics measured **YOLO26n-pose dropping from 56.7 to 33.4 mAP in INT8**, and INT8 was *slower* than FP16 on that GPU ([Ultralytics TensorRT](https://docs.ultralytics.com/integrations/tensorrt/)). The likely explanation, offered as interpretation rather than a sourced claim, is that keypoint regression needs sub-pixel precision that coarse INT8 activation ranges destroy. Joint angles amplify this: a few pixels of error on a short forearm segment shifts the angle by several degrees. INT8 calibration also needs **at least 500 representative images**, and without a `data` YAML Ultralytics falls back to four coco8 images, which are "only enough to test the export" ([Ultralytics TensorRT](https://docs.ultralytics.com/integrations/tensorrt/)). Ship FP16. The Orin Nano also has no DLA cores, so DLA offload is not an option ([Ultralytics Jetson](https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/nvidia-jetson/)).

Smoothing deserves a deliberate choice. A fixed EMA with α = 0.3 adds about **2.3 frames, or roughly 78 ms of lag at 30 FPS (calc)**, which nearly doubles the edge latency budget. The **One-Euro filter** adapts its cutoff to joint speed: it smooths heavily during holds and lightly during fast reps ([Casiez et al., CHI 2012](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00670496)). The authors' tuning procedure is to start with beta = 0 and min_cutoff ≈ 1 Hz, lower min_cutoff if slow movements jitter, and raise beta if fast movements lag ([1€ filter page](https://cristal.univ-lille.fr/~casiez/1euro)). YOLO-pose predicts each frame independently, so apply One-Euro to its keypoints before computing angles.

The pattern is consistent across these studies. A well-placed camera viewing a sagittal-plane movement comes within about **4–8° of motion capture** at the hip and knee. Error grows at the elbow, in the frontal plane, under occlusion, with off-axis views and in unstable tasks. In those conditions, agreement can collapse to near-zero ICC. The geometric reason is that a 2D image-plane angle equals the true joint angle only when both limb segments lie parallel to the sensor. MediaPipe's 3D world landmarks reduce, but do not remove, this foreshortening, because their depth is still estimated from a single camera. The ETH result has a practical consequence: the error is **proportional**, growing with flexion, so a constant offset correction will not fix it. A per-joint linear calibration against a goniometer is required. The app should therefore present its angles as screening and trend measures, not as a replacement for a goniometer. The calibration protocol is straightforward. Mount the camera perpendicular to the plane of motion at joint height, 2–3 m away, with the whole body in frame. Reject frames whose landmark visibility falls below a threshold. Measure a few fixed goniometer angles and report bias, Bland-Altman limits of agreement and ICC. No head-to-head comparison of MediaPipe and YOLO-pose joint-angle accuracy against motion capture was found, so running that comparison would be a genuine contribution of this project.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "can a Jetson run pose estimation?" to "where does the rest of the pipeline lose the Jetson's speed?" At 4.5 ms per frame on the GPU, the ~45–80 ms edge latency budget is consumed mostly by camera buffering, Python glue and smoothing filters, and every item in the error catalogue above can silently undo the hardware advantage. Five configuration choices therefore matter more than any further model choice: a latest-frame capture thread, a verified MAXN SUPER mode, a TensorRT FP16 engine built on the device itself, One-Euro filtering instead of a heavy EMA, and logging tegrastats next to FPS. The same logic applies to privacy. Edge deployment reduces DPDP exposure only if the single cloud touchpoint, the Gemini call, carries no identifiers. A paid tier, or a local template fallback, is a cheap way to remove that residual risk before the fiduciary duties commence in May 2027.

The largest open gap is also the project's best opportunity. No published source gives YOLOv8n-pose or MediaPipe Pose FPS on an Orin Nano, measured board power for a pose workload, end-to-end latency for a Wi-Fi phone camera, or a head-to-head MediaPipe-versus-YOLO joint-angle validation against a goniometer. The student can fill all four with tools already on the board: `yolo benchmark model=yolov8n-pose.pt data=coco8-pose.yaml imgsz=640 half=True device=0`, `tegrastats`, per-stage timestamps, and a goniometer session with Bland-Altman analysis. Measured, they would turn this report's calculated estimates into evidence and give the project a defensible original contribution beyond the integration work.
